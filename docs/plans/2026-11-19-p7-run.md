## Goal

At the Run stage the agent handles the brand's agreed jobs by itself, inside its limits, with every step logged and
undoable where the tool allows, and Pause stops everything.

**Success measure (the real slice):** an allowed restock runs by itself and shows in Activity; a refund still waits.

## Scope

In:
- **The Run policy**, registered on P6's policy hook, active only when the server's stage is Run. It decides twice.
  *At the request:* the action's operation is on the brand's job list and marked eligible for Run in P6's operation
  table, its units are valid, and it reserves its place in the limits atomically in D1; then the system approves that
  exact revision and the 10-second hold starts. *At the claim:* in the same conditional update, the stage is still
  Run, the job is still listed, the action still fits the current limits and this revision still holds its
  reservation. Money and customer actions always wait for a person.
- **Handled jobs:** each logged in Activity and the audit log with Undo where the tool allows (a compensating action
  through the outbox), and Redo (`redoHandled`) where the contract has it.
- **Pause at Run:** P3's Pause (runs) and P6's Pause (the outbox) are proven together at Run; the Pause switch in the
  UI reaches both.
- **Run settings:** the job list and limits on the Inhouse team's pages, read-only for admins, with `ifRev` and audit
  lines.
- A Run-stage eval set: tool data that pushes the agent past its limits or off its job list.
- **The soak's driver and evidence query** (P8 runs the soak and owns its record). They land here because the soak
  starts at this phase's house release and the nightly workflow runs `main`'s code.

Out: new connectors or operations (each joins the operation table when a brand needs it); Pause itself (built in P3 and
P6).

## Design notes

- **A job** is an operation key from the table plus its units field (for a restock, the inventory adjustment's
  `units`).
- **Units are checked before anything is reserved.** A restock's `units` is a positive whole number, within a
  per-SKU bound from the Run settings. A negative or zero adjustment isn't a restock and waits for a person.
- **Who approves at Run.** When the request check passes, a system principal records the approval with its own audit
  actor, and the same 10-second hold applies, so Undo works before the claim exactly as at Act.
- **The limits, defined.** The daily action limit counts actions, one per handled job, in the brand's day in its time
  zone. The largest-restock limit bounds one action's `units`; it isn't a running total. A reservation belongs to one
  intent revision and counts on the day it was made, so a hold that crosses midnight stays on that day.
- **When a reservation is released.** On Undo before the claim, on a Pause's lapse, when the claim check fails, and
  on a definite refusal from the tool. It's kept on `sent`, and on `needs_check` until a person resolves it ("it
  didn't go out" releases it).
- **When the claim check fails** (the stage, the list or a limit changed during the hold), nothing is sent: the
  reservation is released and the action goes back to waiting for a person.
- **Compensating actions.** One kind is exempt from the limits: the server-derived exact inverse of a confirmed send
  (the same SKU, the opposite `units`), once per original action, audited as such. Anything else, a Redo included,
  goes through the policy and the limits as they stand now.
- **Pause** stops new runs, live runs and claims. It can't recall a request the tool already has; that action
  finishes or becomes `needs_check`.
- **At Act**, the same action always waits, whatever the job list says.
- **No edit to the claim.** W1 registers the policy through `app/server/outbox/policy.ts` (P6) and owns nothing under
  `app/server/outbox/`.
- **Wave ownership.** W2 owns `src/agent/mock.ts` and the settings pages in this wave; W3 hands its changes to W2.
  P8's final track edits the settings pages only after this phase is on `main`.
- The policy design is written by the orchestrator (Opus) into W1's brief. Every migration PR adds its table registry
  entry (`app/ops/tables/<resource>.ts`).

## Acceptance

1. At Run, a job inside its limits runs by itself after the hold; one outside the list, past a limit or with invalid
   units waits; money and customer actions always wait.
2. At Act, with the same job list, the same action waits.
3. Limit reservations are atomic: parallel requests never exceed the daily limit, and no single action exceeds the
   largest-restock limit (a concurrency test); a hold across midnight counts on the day it reserved.
4. A reservation is released on Undo, a Pause's lapse, a failed claim check and a definite refusal, and on nothing
   else; a stage, list or limit change during the hold stops the send at the claim.
5. Pause stops new runs, live runs and the outbox at the claim; nothing sends while paused.
6. Every handled job is in Activity and the audit log with the system approval actor; Undo and Redo work where the
   tool allows and are absent where it doesn't; only the exact inverse of a confirmed send skips the limits, once;
   Redo goes through the current policy.
7. P6's property suite passes with the Run policy registered.
8. The Run-stage eval set has zero limit or list bypasses; results are kept.
9. API specs and the contract suite pass for P7 methods; the demo is unchanged.
10. The soak's driver and evidence query run clean once against the test build, and the nightly's soak step is on
    `main` before the release.
11. QA PASS and the ship recording on S1 to S7; the live check passed against the write store; after the release, the
    real-model smoke passes on the house deployment.

## Must not

No action outside the job list or limits; no money or customer action without a person; no auto-run below the Run
stage; no send while paused; no limit enforced only in the UI; no blanket exemption for compensating actions; no edit
to the outbox's claim.

## Risks

- Limits under concurrency: one conditional update per reservation, and a property test over interleavings.
- Undo semantics differ per tool; the operation table states it, and the UI shows Undo only where it's real.
- Two checks can drift apart; both read the same policy function, and the claim's is part of the one conditional
  update.
